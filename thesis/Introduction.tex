In 2024, 79\% of Irish secondary school students progressed to college after graduation \cite{hea2024key} \cite{gov2024department}.
The students who have considered progressing to college have all been posed with the question:
\emph{What course should I study?} 
This is a daunting question for many reasons.
The majority of secondary school students are faced with making this decision 
at 16-19 years old, an age with limited life experience and knowledge about the world.
This decision comes with many personally confronting questions, which may include:
\emph{"What do I want to do with the rest of my life?"} and \emph{"What do I love doing?"}, among many others.
There also exists the fear of choosing a college course the student regrets - 
in 2018, 31\% of recent Irish college graduates reported they were not likely to study their college course again \cite{hea2018graduate}, 
adding more weight to this crucial life decision.\\

Choosing a college course is not only a challenging decision to wrestle with as an individual.
Students are shown to be influenced by a multitude of external factors when choosing their college course.
For instance, parents are one of the most influential factors on a student's college course choice \cite{oya2015university}. 
In addition to this, an individual's gender, socioeconomic status and their parent's educational background
are among the most prominent societal influences on an individual's college course choice \cite{oya2015university}, 
adding complexity to a decision that is already challenging.\\

To assist students in this challenging decision, it is worth introducing the role of 
a Guidance Counsellor (GC).
GC's are trained professional in Irish secondary schools, primarily known for assisting students in their career prospects after graduation.
It is worth noting that the role of a GC extends well beyond vocational support - they are also trained to provide 
educational, personal and social support \cite{igc2016core}.
% make this flow a bit better rather than "jumping out"
The Institute of Guidance Counsellors (IGC) \footnote{\url{https://igc.ie/}} is the professional body for GC's in Ireland.
In a 2022 survey of GC's across Ireland, mental health issues were more commonly encountered compared to
career decision-making issues \footnote{In a 2024 GC survey, career decision-making issues did surpass mental health issues as the most commonly encountered issue for GC's. \cite{igc2024survey}} \cite{igc2022survey}.
In the IGC's 2024-2027 strategic plan \cite{igc2024plan}, the IGC noted this stronger prevalence of mental health issues compared to career decision-making issues,
reflecting their change in strategy for GC's going forward and
signalling a shift in priorities for GC's across Irish secondary schools: vocational support may not be the most important aspect to the role of a GC going forward.\\

We can shift our focus to the more technical side of things. A Recommender System (RS) is a technology that recommend items to a user.
Well-known examples of RS's include Netflix \footnote{\url{https://www.netflix.com/}} recommending films to their customers, or Amazon \footnote{\url{https://www.amazon.com/}} suggesting products to their customers.
One of the earliest known RS's was \emph{Tapestry} in 1992,
a system that made tailored mailing list recommendations to its users \cite{goldberg1992using}. 
Around this time, the field of RS's received considerable interest as e-commerce websites could increase
their revenue if they suggested more personalised products to their users.
Amazon and Netflix are the most notable adopters of RS's
in order to increase company revenue \cite{schafer1999recommender}.
There are a wide variety of approaches to recommending items to users, although, two very common types of RS's are \emph{Collaborative Filtering} and \emph{Content-based Filtering} RS's. 
\emph{Collaborative Filtering} recommends items to users based on what other similar users also liked, whereas
\emph{Content-based Filtering} recommends items to users based on the user's preferences \cite{aggarwal2016recommender}.\\

As previously stated, an RS recommends \emph{items} to a \emph{user}.
However, we can re-conceptualize an RS in the context of college courses:
\emph{college courses} can take the place of \emph{items}, and a \emph{student} can take the place of a \emph{user}.
Given the established difficulty in choosing a suitable college course, the shifting priorities of GC's towards student mental health as opposed to career-making decisions,
and a technology that could be appropriately applied to this domain,
an exciting opportunity presents itself: A Recommender System for Irish College Courses.\\

A technology, such as an RS, could aid a student in navigating a difficult decision-making process.
For instance, there are over 45 higher education institutes in Ireland offering a total of over 1700 courses \footnote{Higher education colleges and courses can be found here: \textbf{TODO}}, %\url{https://careersportal.ie/courses/coursefinder}
representing a huge set of choice to the student.
An advantage of a personalised RS is to provide a user with a much smaller list of relevant recommendations.
\cite{long2025choice} demonstrated that smaller recommendation sets are often equally as effective as larger recommendations sets,
suggesting that a much narrower set of personalised college course recommendations could be effective in this space.\\

It is worth noting that there are no intentions behind this RS to replace the career guidance role of a GC.
As mentioned previously, choosing a college course is a difficult and personally confronting decision.
GC's are trained to additionally support a student's social and personal needs \cite{igc2016core},
allowing the GC to provide a student with empathetic and human-centric support - 
an aspect that a traditional RS would fail to effectively convey.\\

Furthermore, there are no intentions behind this RS to entirely replace the student's college course decision-making process.
As we will discover in Chapter \ref{chapter:literature-review}, the decision of a college course is incredibly nuanced.
It is the author's opinion that no system can or should make that decision entirely for an individual.\\

Rather, the intention behind this RS is to \emph{complement} the student's college course decision-making process.
In the case of a college course RS, the author would argue that an effective RS would assist a student in exploring 
a variety of career paths that may be relevant to them.\\

This ultimately describes the motivation behind this research.

\section{Research Question}

In light of the motivation behind this research, the Research Question for this dissertation can be formulated:
\emph{How can a Recommender System assist an individual in their college course decision-making process?}
