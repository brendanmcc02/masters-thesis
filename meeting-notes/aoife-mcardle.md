# Her guidance counselling process
* she thinks guidance should be extremely **non-directive**
* her role is to suggest lots of things, gets the students to do their own research
    * it's **student-centric,** she is just there to give support in their journey
* she mentioned that we shouldn't be using the word **"recommendations"**, because there's an implication that the student *should* do the course
    * what she does is make **"suggestions"** -> acknowledging the potential there
    * **she doesn't advise, she supports**
* doesn't like talking about careers, in fact she is allergic to the the idea of "career guidance"
    * the majority of jobs that will exist in the future do not exist today
* guidance has moved on from careers, this being one reason
* careers aren't linear

# Personality tests
* we don't deal with ocean/mbti
* there was some careers thing careers4u? -> aptitude tests, personality tests, etc. she found it destructive
* she finds the personality tests can put labels on people -> pidgeon hole, kids wouldn't have the maturity to recognise the nuance of these labels, and they would infer and put themselves in that box
* does more harm than good

# What other information do you ask them?
* ask about their **favourite subjects/most challenging** and **why**
    * she said that you can find out a lot from that
* also asks them what they do in their spare time, what do they read or are interested in
    * gave an example of a student that lives/breathes politics
* she thinks that [hobbies](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/muhadel/hobbies) can really inform what people what want

# Guidance Counselling
* I didn't know this, but a guidance counsellor does a lot more than just figuring out what students want to do out of secondary school
* there are 3 strands to guidance counselling:
    1. vocational
        * which is my area of focus
    2. educational
        * study skills, subject choices, etc.
    3. social/personal
        * pastoral care, important but not relevant to my thesis

# Socio-Economic Background
* really interesting, in my research I was garnering this idea that people with higher SES have more tendency to do looser courses and prioritise things other than money
    * her experience is the complete opposite
    * she asked an entire class if they would prefer a job that makes lots of money that they don't like, or a job that they love but doesn't make a lot of money
    * in her school (rathdown, glenageary), everyone said they want the job that makes money
    * they are acutely aware of economic reality and aren't naive
* she does volunteer work once per week in a DEIS school
    * when you're from lower SES, there is less awareness of the kinds of jobs out there
    * for example, they may not really know what an economist does, etc.
    * which can be limiting and pushes them into jobs that they parents are aware of, e.g. trades
* whereas when you're from a higher SES, the parents generally have a lot of different connections with people
    * so they would have more awareness of other jobs, which broadens their kids choices
